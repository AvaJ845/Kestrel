How it works

The same statistical engine, ported to run on your device.

  • Baseline. Kestrel seeds each station from its recent hourly readings, weighting the most recent hours most heavily.
  • Score. It computes a time-decay weighted z-score of the current temperature against that baseline — how many standard deviations from normal.
  • Cross-validate. An independent METAR sensor confirms the reading; disagreement lowers confidence.
  • Rank. Cities sort by anomaly strength, so the most unusual conditions rise to the top.

The honesty pledge

Kestrel produces statistical anomaly scores from public weather data — not forecasts, not predictions, and not financial, trading, or betting advice. A high score means “unusual right now,” never “what happens next.” It has no connection to any market, exchange, or wager.
